Most brands we review aren't trying to mislead anyone. They have a product that works and research they believe in. Then the page gets written on a deadline and the wording drifts. Supports becomes fixes. May help becomes cures. Nobody in the room can say where the line sits, because it lives in FTC guidance and a section of the Code of Federal Regulations no marketing team has a reason to read.

Our job is to tell you what your evidence supports and where the copy has moved past it. Whether something creates legal liability is a lawyer's question, and when a call turns on that, we say so and stop.

Why a neuroscientist reads your copy

The FTC's standard is competent and reliable scientific evidence. Deciding whether a cell-culture study, an ingredient-level meta-analysis, or a twelve-person open-label trial clears that bar is a scientific call, not a legal one. I've been through peer review as an author and had research funded by federal review panels, which means I've sat on both sides of people deciding whether evidence holds up.

My undergraduate degree was in integrative neuroscience — psychology, biology, and chemistry together — which matters more than I expected for the wellness side of this work. Rewires your brain. Boosts dopamine. Regulates your vagus nerve. Those are neuroscience claims, and knowing whether the research says what the copy implies means having read the research rather than a summary of it.

The editorial background is why the rewrites are usable. Most compliance review hands you red ink and a wooden replacement line, which is how findings end up quietly ignored. I've worked as an editor. What comes back is meant to ship.

What we check against

FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance. The structure/function rules in 21 CFR 101.93. The FTC Endorsement Guides and the rule on fake reviews. Meta, Google, and Amazon's published policies. And for mechanism claims, the underlying literature itself.

What we won't do

Give legal advice, represent you before an agency, tell you whether something creates liability, or certify you compliant. Nobody can do that last one. When a call turns on legal interpretation or on licensing rules in your state, we'll say that's where it sits and stop.

How we're set up

A master services agreement for ongoing work, or a short simple one for a single project. Confidentiality in both directions. Every review is read and signed by the lead reviewer — nothing is delegated to a junior or a template.

ClaimFacts provides scientific and regulatory review of marketing claims. Reviews are conducted by Haris Hillery, M.S., Founder and Lead Reviewer. It is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice or legal representation. Reviews interpret published FTC guidance, FDA labeling regulations, and platform policy; they do not constitute an opinion on legal liability and do not create an attorney-client relationship. Consult qualified counsel before relying on any review for regulated filings, agency correspondence, or litigation.
